2027 isn't a generous year for Swedish long weekends. May Day (1 May) and All Saints' Day (6 November) fall on Saturdays, National Day (6 June) is a Sunday, and Christmas Day and Boxing Day 2027 are a Saturday and a Sunday. Sweden doesn't move holidays that land on a weekend, so those are simply lost.

What remains are three good long weekends, plus whatever you build with leave. This guide covers trips from Stockholm for those weekends that don't need a flight: trains to the other Nordic capitals, overnight ferries across the Baltic, and the islands closer to home.

## The 2027 long weekends

| Weekend | Days off | Leave days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Easter: Fri 26 – Mon 29 March | 4 | none |
| Ascension: Thu 6 – Sun 9 May | 4 | Fri 7 May (1) |
| Midsummer: Fri 25 – Sun 27 June | 3 | none |

Midsummer Eve isn't a public holiday by law, but nearly every workplace treats it as one. Each weekend can be stretched with a few leave days; the planner for [Sweden 2027](/se/2027/) shows how far your own allowance goes. We have separate guides for [Ascension](/guides/ascension-day-2027-bridge-day-trips/) and [Midsummer](/guides/midsummer-2027-sweden/).

Travel times below are typical and change with timetables and engineering works, which are common on holiday weekends. Check them when you book.

## By train

### Gothenburg: about three hours

The high-speed trains between Stockholm and Gothenburg take around three hours, which makes Gothenburg the easiest long weekend in Sweden. Over Easter and in early May it's a city weekend: the Haga and Linné districts, fish and seafood at the Feskekôrka market hall and around the harbour, and the southern archipelago a tram and a short ferry ride away (the archipelago ferries are part of the city's public transport). By Midsummer, the islands are the main reason to go.

### Copenhagen: about five hours

Direct trains from Stockholm to Copenhagen take around five hours, or a little more with a change in Malmö. Copenhagen is the best city weekend within train range: Tivoli usually opens for its spring season around Easter, and by Ascension the harbour, the cycling and the outdoor restaurants are in full swing. Malmö is a good alternative if you want something quieter, and it's 35 minutes from Copenhagen over the Öresund Bridge, so you can combine the two.

### Oslo: about five to six hours

There are few direct trains from Stockholm to Oslo, and track works sometimes replace part of the journey with buses, so check the route carefully. When it runs smoothly, it's a pleasant ride through Värmland. Oslo at Easter is still wintry, and many Norwegians leave for the mountains over påske, so the city is calm. In May and June, the waterfront walk from the Opera House and the Munch museum at Bjørvika to Aker Brygge makes a good weekend on foot, as does a boat trip on the Oslofjord.

### Further: Hamburg and Berlin

Hamburg and Berlin are possible in a long weekend by train, either by day with changes in Copenhagen and Hamburg (around ten to twelve hours to Hamburg), or on the night train to Berlin via Hamburg that has run in recent years. Its timetable has changed several times, so check that it runs on your dates before you plan around it. For a four-day weekend this only works if you're happy to spend two of the nights on a train.

## By overnight ferry

The overnight ferries across the Baltic are the Stockholm trip that does the most with a long weekend: you leave in the late afternoon, sleep on board, and arrive the next morning with no time lost. Book a cabin; on holiday weekends the cheaper cabins sell out first.

### Helsinki

The ferries to Helsinki leave Stockholm in the late afternoon and arrive the following morning. On a four-day Thursday-to-Sunday weekend you could leave on Wednesday evening if you can, or Thursday, spend a day and a half in Helsinki, and take the ferry back on Saturday evening to arrive on Sunday morning. Helsinki highlights include the public saunas (Löyly by the sea, the Allas Sea Pool by the market square) and the fortress island of Suomenlinna, a short ferry ride from the centre.

### Tallinn

The overnight ferry to Tallinn takes a little longer than to Helsinki, and the same pattern applies: leave in the afternoon, arrive in the morning. Tallinn's medieval old town is one of the best preserved in Northern Europe and is easily seen on foot in a day; spend the second day in the Kalamaja and Telliskivi districts, or at the Kadriorg park and palace. Tallinn is also an easy ferry ride from Helsinki, around two hours, so you can combine the two cities on a longer break.

### Turku and Åland

Ferries to Turku stop at Mariehamn on Åland, and there are day and night sailings. Åland on its own makes a gentle weekend in late spring and at Midsummer: flat cycling between islands, red granite and small villages. It's Finnish but Swedish-speaking, and the shortest crossing is from Grisslehamn, north of Stockholm, at around two hours.

## Closer to home

### Gotland

The ferry from Nynäshamn to Visby takes around three hours. Gotland in early May is quiet and green, with Visby's town walls and medieval ruins mostly to yourself; Midsummer is the start of the summer season, with many more visitors and a much busier ferry. Book cars on the ferry early for both weekends.

### The Stockholm archipelago

Waxholmsbolaget boats from Strömkajen in central Stockholm reach Vaxholm in about an hour, and islands like Grinda, Möja and Utö in a few hours. The spring timetable is thinner than the summer one, which normally starts in June, and many island restaurants and hostels open only for the summer season. At Easter and Ascension, pick an island with year-round accommodation, or go for a day; at Midsummer, the archipelago is where much of Stockholm goes.

## Practical tips

1. **Book train tickets as soon as they're on sale.** The cheapest fares on the busiest days, the Thursday before Easter and the Wednesday before Ascension, go first.
2. **Bring ID on the ferries.** Ferries between Sweden and Finland or Estonia stay within the Schengen area, but the ferry companies ask for identification, and you need it for any border checks.
3. **Eat before you board or book the buffet.** The restaurants on the ferries fill up quickly on departure evenings.
4. **Check the seasons.** Many islands, both in the archipelago and on Åland, are quiet with limited services until June.

## Our pick

Easter: Copenhagen by train, or Tallinn by overnight ferry. Ascension: Gothenburg and its archipelago, or Helsinki with a sauna evening. Midsummer: Gotland or an archipelago island, booked in winter.
