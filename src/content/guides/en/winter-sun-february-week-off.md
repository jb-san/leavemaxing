There are no public holidays in February in Sweden or Germany, so a week of winter sun costs a full week of leave: five days for nine days off, from one Saturday to the Sunday after next. That makes it worth choosing the destination carefully. A lot of places sold as "winter sun" are mild rather than warm in February, and the difference between 16 °C and 22 °C is the difference between a city break with a jacket and lying by a pool.

This guide gives realistic February conditions, flight times from Sweden and Germany, and the school-holiday weeks that drive prices.

## The school holiday weeks that set the prices

**Sweden.** Sportlov, the winter sports week, is set by each municipality and spread over weeks 7 to 10. In 2027 those are:

| Week | Dates |
| --- | --- |
| 7 | 15 – 21 February |
| 8 | 22 – 28 February |
| 9 | 1 – 7 March |
| 10 | 8 – 14 March |

Stockholm usually takes week 9, Gothenburg week 7 and Malmö week 8, with much of Norrland later, but municipalities change this, so check yours. Package trips to the Canaries and flights to the Alps are at their most expensive in the week your region is off, and noticeably cheaper the week before or after. If you don't have school-age children, avoid the week of your nearest big city.

**Germany.** Winter holidays depend on the state. Some states have a week or so in February (often around carnival; Rosenmontag is 8 February 2027), some have only a day or two between school terms, and some have none. Check your state's school calendar before you book, both to plan around it and because the states with a February week push up prices to the Canaries and the Alps on those dates.

Whichever country you're in, the cost in leave is the same: five days for a Saturday-to-Sunday week. You can see what else those days could do in the planner for [Sweden 2027](/se/2027/) or [Germany 2027](/de/2027/).

The pattern is simple: in February, Europe proper tops out around 18 °C. If you want to swim without gritting your teeth, you need to go to the latitude of the Canaries or further south.

## The destinations, honestly

### Canary Islands: the reliable choice

The Canaries are the safest bet for warmth in Europe in February, and they're within the EU, so no extra paperwork. The southern coasts of Gran Canaria and Tenerife are the warmest and sunniest; the north of each island is greener, cloudier and cooler. Lanzarote and Fuerteventura are slightly cooler and windy, which kitesurfers love and sunbathers don't. Expect most days to be pleasant, a few to be cloudy, and evenings that need a jumper.

Sportlov weeks are peak season on the Canaries for both Swedes and Germans, so they book out early. If your dates are fixed by school, book in autumn.

### Madeira: for walking rather than lying on a beach

Madeira is a little cooler and wetter than the Canaries, especially on the north side of the island, and it has few sandy beaches. What it does have is some of the best walking in Europe in winter: the levada paths along old irrigation channels, many easy and nearly flat, and the steep ridge walks between the highest peaks. Choose Madeira for active holidays, not for a week by the pool.

### Red Sea: the warmest option with short flights

Hurghada, Marsa Alam and Sharm el-Sheikh are warmer than the Canaries in February and have the best snorkelling and diving within six hours of Northern Europe. Days are warm but it can be windy, and desert nights are cool. Package holidays dominate here; all-inclusive resorts are good value outside the sportlov weeks. Check the entry rules for Egypt (most EU citizens buy a visa on arrival or online) and current travel advice from your foreign ministry before you book.

### Cape Verde: real heat, longer flights

Sal and Boa Vista are around 25 °C in February with warm sea and constant trade winds. Flights are mostly charters and take around six to seven hours. Good for beach and wind sports; less so for sightseeing.

### Cyprus and Malta: mild, not hot

Both are excellent in February for walking, history and food, with far fewer visitors than in summer and lower prices. But they are mild, not warm: 15–18 °C, with some rain. Malta in particular is a great city-and-culture week (Valletta, Mdina, Gozo) rather than a beach week. Cyprus can surprise you: on a clear day you can be on the coast in the morning and in snow in the Troodos mountains in the afternoon.

### Southern Spain and the Algarve: sunny, but bring a jacket

Málaga, Seville and the Algarve have a lot of sunshine in February and almond trees in blossom, but the temperatures are spring-like, around 16–18 °C. They're very good for an active week of walking, golf or city sightseeing, and often cheap outside school weeks.

### Long-haul: Thailand, the Caribbean, Dubai

With only nine days, long-haul is possible but costs you most of two days in travel. Dubai is the shortest of them at around six hours, warm and dry in February. Thailand and the Caribbean are better with a second week.

## How to book February well

1. **Pick the week before the destination.** Prices move more with school holiday weeks than with the destination. If you're flexible, compare your sportlov week with the one either side of it.
2. **Compare packages with flights plus hotel.** For the Canaries, Red Sea and Cape Verde, charter packages with flights from regional airports are often the cheapest, especially for families. For Madeira, Malta and Cyprus, building the trip yourself is often more flexible.
3. **Book the return for Sunday if you can.** It gives you nine days off for five leave days, and Sunday returns are usually cheaper than Saturday ones in sportlov weeks.
4. **Check the luggage.** A winter-sun trip from a cold country means packing for two climates. Wear the heavy jacket onto the plane and pack the shorts.

## Our pick

For warmth with the least risk: the south of Gran Canaria or Tenerife, in a week that isn't your region's sportlov week. For warmth plus the sea: the Red Sea. For walking and food at a lower price: Madeira, Malta or Cyprus, with a jacket.
