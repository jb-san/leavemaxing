May 2027 is the best month of the year for leave in Germany. After Ascension on Thursday 6 May comes Pentecost (Pfingsten), with Whit Sunday on 16 May and **Whit Monday (Pfingstmontag) on 17 May**, and then in six states **Corpus Christi (Fronleichnam) on Thursday 27 May**. This guide covers the dates, the states, the combinations worth booking and where to go in late May.

## Which days are holidays where

Whit Monday is a public holiday in every German state. Whit Sunday is a Sunday anyway (Brandenburg lists it as a holiday too, which makes no practical difference).

Corpus Christi is a holiday only in the states with large Catholic populations:

- [Baden-Württemberg](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/baden-wuerttemberg/)
- [Bavaria](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/bayern/)
- [Hesse](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/hessen/)
- [North Rhine-Westphalia](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/nordrhein-westfalen/)
- [Rhineland-Palatinate](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/rheinland-pfalz/)
- [Saarland](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/saarland/)

It's also a holiday in some Catholic communities in Saxony and Thuringia, but not in those states as a whole. If you work in a different state from the one you live in, what counts is where you work. The state pages linked above have every 2027 holiday and bridge day for that state; the national overview is in the [planner for Germany 2027](/de/2027/).

If you live in a Corpus Christi state, the 16-day break from 15 to 30 May for eight leave days is one of the best deals in the German calendar. Add Ascension's bridge day on 7 May and you've had three long breaks in a month.

In Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg, the school Whitsun holidays (Pfingstferien) usually cover the time around Pentecost, so families from those states travel then and prices rise accordingly. Check your state's school calendar if that matters to you.

## Where to go in late May

Late May is one of the best times of year to travel in Europe. The Mediterranean is warm, typically 23–27 °C in the daytime, without the heat and crowds of July and August. The sea is warming up but still fresh: around 19–21 °C in most of the Mediterranean, cooler on the Atlantic.

Keep in mind that Whit Monday is a holiday in much of Europe too (Austria, most of Switzerland, France, the Benelux countries, Denmark and Norway among them), and Corpus Christi in Austria, Poland, Croatia and Portugal, so popular destinations close to Germany are busy with neighbours on the same long weekend.

### For four days (Corpus Christi weekend)

- **Lake Garda and Verona.** A classic for southern Germans, around five and a half hours by train from Munich to Verona, with Lake Garda a short bus ride on. It's very busy over the Pentecost and Corpus Christi weekends.
- **Vienna, Salzburg, Prague.** Vienna is about four hours by train from Munich and Prague about four and a half from Berlin. Note that Corpus Christi is a holiday in Austria too, so Vienna will be busy.
- **Paris, Amsterdam, Brussels.** Easy by train from Cologne and Frankfurt, under four hours. In the Netherlands and Belgium, Corpus Christi is a normal working day.
- **Mallorca and Barcelona.** About two hours by plane, warm and long-lit in late May. Mallorca over Pentecost is very popular with Germans, so book early.

### For nine days or more

- **Croatia's coast.** Istria or the Dalmatian islands in late May are green, warm and calm; ferries between the islands run more often from June, so check timetables if you plan to hop. Remember that Corpus Christi is a holiday in Croatia too.
- **Sardinia or Corsica.** Beaches without the summer crowds, and good walking. The ferries from Genoa and Livorno make a road trip possible, if you're driving.
- **Greece.** Crete, Rhodes, the Peloponnese or the Ionian islands. Late May is ideal for walking and sightseeing, and warm enough for the beach.
- **Portugal.** Lisbon and Porto in late May are lively; the Algarve is warm, and its sea is cooler than the Mediterranean.
- **Scotland or Norway.** If you prefer cool weather and long days, late May has some of the driest weather of the year in many parts of Scotland and very long evenings in Norway. EU citizens need an ETA for the UK, which you should apply for in advance.

### Closer to home

- **The Baltic Sea.** Usedom, Rügen and the Fischland-Darß peninsula are quiet before the summer season, with long beaches, although the sea is still cold, often 12–15 °C.
- **The Alps.** Lower valleys and lakes are beautiful in late May, but many high trails and mountain huts are still snowed in or closed until June. Plan valley walks and lake circuits rather than summit tours.
- **Wine regions.** The Moselle, the Rheingau, the Palatinate and Franconia are at their greenest, and the wine festival season begins.

## Practical tips

1. **Expect traffic.** Friday afternoons before Pentecost and Corpus Christi, and the evenings of Whit Monday and the Sunday after Corpus Christi, are among the busiest on German motorways, especially heading south. If you drive, leave early or late.
2. **Book trains early.** Deutsche Bahn's cheapest fares sell out on the busiest days, and trains on holiday weekends are full.
3. **Book the accommodation first in popular spots.** Lake Garda, Mallorca and the Baltic islands fill up for Pentecost weekend long before the flights do.
4. **Check opening hours.** In Corpus Christi states, the processions and closed shops affect towns on the Thursday; abroad, the holidays differ by country.

## Our pick

If you live in a Corpus Christi state: book 18–21 May, 24–26 May and 28 May for a 16-day break and go somewhere you need time for, such as Greece, Sardinia or a Croatian island trip. Everywhere else: 18–21 May for a nine-day Pentecost break, or combine Ascension and Pentecost for 12 days with six leave days.
