The 2026/27 holidays fall well in Sweden. Christmas Eve is a Thursday, New Year's Eve is a Thursday and Epiphany (trettondedag jul) is a Wednesday, so a handful of leave days opens up a long stretch in the darkest part of the year. This guide shows the ways to shape the break and which kinds of trips suit each one.

## The dates, and how many leave days they take

Christmas Eve (julafton) and New Year's Eve (nyårsafton) aren't public holidays in Swedish law, but most workplaces treat them as days off, and the planner counts them that way. If yours doesn't, add a day to each option below.

| Break | Days off | Leave days to book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thu 24 Dec 2026 – Sun 3 Jan 2027 | 11 | 28, 29 and 30 December (3) |
| Thu 31 Dec 2026 – Sun 10 Jan 2027 | 11 | 4, 5, 7 and 8 January (4) |
| Thu 24 Dec 2026 – Wed 6 Jan 2027 | 14 | 28–30 December, 4–5 January (5) |
| Thu 24 Dec 2026 – Sun 10 Jan 2027 | 18 | 28–30 December, 4–5 and 7–8 January (7) |

The first option is the bargain: three leave days for eleven days off, because Christmas Day is a Friday and New Year's Day is a Friday too. The second costs one day more, skips Christmas and gives you the first week of January, when flights and hotels are usually calmer than in the week between the holidays. The 18-day version is what people who save leave all year tend to take, and it is long enough for a long-haul trip without feeling rushed.

You can check these against your own allowance in the planner for [Sweden 2026](/se/2026/) and [Sweden 2027](/se/2027/). If you live somewhere with a different leave year, or you want to block days you've already promised to work, mark them there and the plan rebuilds.

## First decide what kind of break you want

Over the holidays the trip type matters more than the destination. Three questions sort most people quickly:

- **Are you spending Christmas Eve at home?** Most Swedes do. Then the second option, from New Year's Eve, or a departure on Christmas Day or Boxing Day, is the realistic one.
- **Do you want light or warmth?** Southern Sweden gets about seven hours of daylight at the turn of the year, Stockholm about six, and the far north none to speak of. If what you're short of is daylight, go south. If you'd rather lean into the winter, go north.
- **How much of the break can go to travel?** Eleven days is enough for a long-haul flight; four or five days is not.

## Canary Islands: the default for a reason

Gran Canaria and Tenerife are the classic Swedish winter escape, and around New Year the numbers explain why: daytime highs usually around 20–22 °C on the southern coasts, sea temperatures around 19–20 °C, and flights of roughly five and a half to six hours from Stockholm. Direct flights run from Arlanda, Landvetter and Copenhagen most of the winter.

Two things to know. First, the holiday weeks are the most expensive weeks of the Canary season, and package trips for the Christmas week often sell out by autumn. If you're booking now, late November, look at leaving on Christmas Day or New Year's Day itself: those departures are often cheaper and emptier because so few people want to travel on them. Second, the islands are not all the same in winter. The south of Gran Canaria (Playa del Inglés, Maspalomas, Puerto Rico) and the south-west of Tenerife are the sunniest; the north of both islands is greener and noticeably cloudier and cooler. Lanzarote and Fuerteventura are a little cooler and often windy.

## Madeira: for walkers and for the fireworks

Madeira is a few degrees cooler than the Canaries, with daytime highs usually around 19 °C in Funchal in late December, and it rains more on the north side of the island. What it has instead is walking: the levada trails that follow old irrigation channels along the mountainsides, many of them easy and almost flat. It also has one of the best-known New Year's Eve fireworks displays in Europe, over the bay in Funchal, and hotels with a view of the harbour book up early for that night. If you go for the fireworks, book the accommodation before the flights.

Flights from Sweden take around five hours and are often via a connection outside the charter season, so compare a direct package with a self-built trip.

## Lapland: lean into the dark

If you'd rather not fly south, the north of Sweden is at its most dramatic at the turn of the year. In Kiruna the sun doesn't rise above the horizon for a few weeks around Christmas; you get a few hours of blue and pink twilight around midday instead, which is beautiful and also very short. Temperatures of −15 °C are normal and much colder spells happen. Northern lights need dark, clear skies, so you have plenty of the first and have to hope for the second; Abisko is popular because it is often clearer than the surrounding area, but no place can promise a sighting.

Practical points: dog sledding, snowmobile tours and stays at the Icehotel in Jukkasjärvi are the big draws, and the holiday weeks are their busiest, so book activities as early as the travel. The night train from Stockholm to Kiruna and Abisko takes roughly 17 hours and is a trip in itself, but sleeper cabins over Christmas and New Year sell out; flights to Kiruna or Luleå take about an hour and a half. Pack properly: wool base layers, a real winter jacket, mittens rather than gloves and boots with room for thick socks. Tour operators usually lend out overalls and boots for their own trips.

## Thailand and other long-haul trips: only with the longer versions

Thailand is a long-standing Swedish winter favourite, and December to February is the dry season on the Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi, Khao Lak). The Gulf side, including Koh Samui, is often still wet in December. Bangkok is around 10–11 hours from Stockholm on a direct flight when one is running, and more like 13–16 hours with a change. Thailand is six hours ahead of Swedish winter time.

Our advice: don't do long-haul on the eleven-day options unless you genuinely don't mind spending two of those days in transit and two more adjusting. The 18-day version is where it starts to feel worth it. Holiday-week fares to Asia are high, and the gap between a well-timed booking and a late one is large, so this is the trip type where booking early matters most. Check the entry rules for your passport before you book; they have changed in recent years.

## A European city: Vienna, Berlin, Budapest or Copenhagen

A city is the best use of a short break inside the long one, for example New Year's Eve to 3 January. A few that work at New Year:

- **Vienna** does New Year's Eve properly, with the Silvesterpfad, a trail of stages and stalls through the inner city, and dancing to Strauss waltzes in the streets at midnight. Tickets for the Vienna Philharmonic's New Year's Concert are allocated by a lottery that opens early in the year, so that one has to be planned twelve months ahead.
- **Berlin** has the big party at the Brandenburg Gate and an enormous amount of private fireworks everywhere else, which some people love and others find chaotic. The museums are open most days between the holidays.
- **Budapest** is good value and has its thermal baths: sitting in the outdoor pools at Széchenyi in winter air is the reason many people go.
- **Copenhagen** is close, easy by train from southern Sweden, and Tivoli usually stays open with its Christmas season into the first days of January.

A warning that catches people every year: in much of Central Europe, Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year's Day are quiet days. Many shops and some museums and restaurants close, especially on 25 December and 1 January. Plan the 1st as a walking and recovering day, and book a table for New Year's Eve in advance, since many restaurants only offer set menus that night.

## Booking from late November

If you haven't booked anything yet, you're late for the Christmas week but not for the break. A few things help:

1. **Move the departure, not the destination.** 24 and 31 December and 1 January are often among the cheapest days to fly in the whole period, because almost nobody wants to. If you can do Christmas Eve lunch and fly in the evening, or fly on New Year's morning, you'll often save a lot.
2. **Search "anywhere" first.** For a fixed set of days, being flexible on where to go gets you much further than being flexible on when. Kiwi.com, Google Flights and Skyscanner all have a search with no destination.
3. **Check Copenhagen.** From Skåne, Kastrup often has more direct routes than Malmö or even Arlanda.
4. **Use the second option if the first is gone.** The 31 December to 10 January break costs one leave day more, and January is usually calmer than the Christmas week.

## A suggested plan

If we had to pick one shape for most people: take 28–30 December off, spend Christmas at home, fly out on 26 or 27 December to the Canaries or Madeira, and come back on 2 January, so Sunday the 3rd is there to unpack before work. If you have the leave for the 18-day version and want something different, that's the year to do Thailand or a proper week in Lapland.

Whatever you choose, put the dates in the [planner](/se/2026/) first and check them with your manager before you book anything that can't be refunded.
