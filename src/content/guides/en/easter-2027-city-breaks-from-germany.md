Easter is early in 2027. Easter Sunday is 28 March, so Good Friday (Karfreitag) is 26 March and Easter Monday (Ostermontag) is 29 March. Both are public holidays in every German state, which makes Friday to Monday a four-day weekend that costs no leave at all. This guide covers how to stretch it, which cities suit a four-day trip in late March, and how to get there from the big German cities.

## The dates and the leave they take

| Break | Days off | Leave days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fri 26 – Mon 29 March | 4 | none |
| Fri 26 March – Sun 4 April | 10 | 30 March – 2 April (4) |
| Sat 20 – Mon 29 March | 10 | 22–25 March (4) |
| Sat 20 March – Sun 4 April | 16 | 22–25 March, 30 March – 2 April (8) |

Four leave days give you ten days off either side of Easter, and eight give you sixteen. Easter school holidays (Osterferien) differ from state to state, so if you travel with children check your state's dates first, then build around them. Without children, the week that is *not* your state's school holiday is usually the calmer and cheaper one.

You can check these dates against your own allowance in the [planner for Germany 2027](/de/2027/). The German pages also have one per state, for example [Bavaria](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/bayern/) or [North Rhine-Westphalia](/de-de/brueckentage-2027/nordrhein-westfalen/), which add the state holidays later in the year.

## What to expect from late March

Late March is early spring in most of Europe. The Mediterranean is mild rather than warm: daytime highs are usually around 17–20 °C in Rome, Lisbon and Barcelona, a little more in Seville and Malaga, and the sea is still too cold for most people to swim. Central and northern cities are somewhere between 8 and 14 °C, with a real chance of rain. Pack a proper jacket wherever you go.

Two things are special about Easter 2027:

- **The clocks change on Easter Sunday.** Summer time in the EU starts on the last Sunday of March, which in 2027 is 28 March. You lose an hour that night, so check the times of any Sunday morning train or flight, and of any Easter Sunday service you plan to attend.
- **Easter is a holiday almost everywhere you might go.** Good Friday and Easter Monday are public holidays in many European countries, and in most of Spain Maundy Thursday is too. Expect closed shops and some closed museums on the holidays themselves, and local crowds on the days in between.

## By train: cities within about four to six hours

For four days, the train is often the better choice: you leave from the city centre, there is no security queue, and you arrive in the middle of the next city. Trains at Easter are busy and engineering works are common on holiday weekends, so book early and check the journey again a week before. Journey times below are typical for direct or one-change trains and vary with the timetable.

**From Berlin**

- **Prague** takes around four and a half hours by EuroCity along the Elbe valley, one of the best train rides in this part of Europe. Prague usually holds Easter markets on the Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square from around mid-March into April.
- **Copenhagen** is a longer trip, about seven hours with a change. It's worth it if you like the city; Tivoli usually opens its spring season around this time of year.
- **Wrocław** is about four to five hours by train (Kraków takes a good deal longer). It is good value, and Polish Easter traditions, such as the blessing of food baskets on Holy Saturday, are very visible.

**From Munich**

- **Vienna** is about four hours by Railjet. The Easter markets at Freyung and in front of Schönbrunn Palace usually run for a few weeks before Easter.
- **Salzburg** is under two hours away, close enough for a relaxed four days with a day trip to the lakes.
- **Zurich** takes around three and a half hours; **Verona** around five and a half over the Brenner pass, with Lake Garda a short bus ride from there.

**From Frankfurt and Cologne**

- **Paris** is about four hours from Frankfurt and three and a half from Cologne on direct high-speed trains.
- **Amsterdam** is about four hours from Frankfurt and under three from Cologne. Keukenhof, the tulip garden near Leiden, usually opens in the second half of March, so it may just be open over Easter 2027; check the season dates before you plan around it, and expect the early weeks to show more crocuses and daffodils than tulips.
- **Brussels** is under two hours from Cologne, and Bruges about an hour beyond it; the two work well together.

**From Hamburg**

- **Copenhagen** is about five hours by train.
- **Amsterdam** takes around five hours with a change.

## By plane: where four days are enough

If you fly, keep it to three hours or less each way and leave on Thursday evening if you can; that turns four days into four full days rather than three and a half. Some good fits for late March:

- **Lisbon** is about three hours from Frankfurt or Munich and one of the mildest capitals in Europe at this time of year. Good for walking, though the hills and cobbles are tiring.
- **Seville** at Easter is a special case. Semana Santa, Holy Week, runs from Palm Sunday (21 March 2027) to Easter Sunday, with processions through the old town day and night. It is one of the great spectacles in Europe, and hotels charge accordingly and book up months ahead. Go for it, not despite it.
- **Rome** is busy at Easter with pilgrims; the Pope usually celebrates Easter Mass in St Peter's Square and gives the *Urbi et Orbi* blessing on Easter Sunday. If you don't want the crowds, see the Vatican on another day and keep Easter Sunday for a different part of the city. The Vatican Museums close on a number of church holidays, so check their calendar before you book tickets.
- **Athens** is a good choice in 2027 for a different reason: Orthodox Easter is a month later, on 2 May 2027, so Athens is in normal spring mode over the Western Easter weekend.

## Making four days work

- **Arrive by lunch on Friday.** Thursday evening departures are ideal, but they are the most expensive of the weekend. A Friday morning train or flight is often the best compromise.
- **Book timed tickets early.** At Easter, the big sights (the Vatican, the Alhambra, the Sagrada Família, the Anne Frank House) sell out. If a sight is the reason for the trip, buy the ticket before the travel.
- **Plan Sunday and Monday carefully.** Easter Sunday is quiet in many countries and Monday is a holiday in many of them, so book restaurants and check opening hours for those two days.
- **Come home on Monday afternoon, not evening.** Monday evening trains and flights back to Germany are among the busiest of the weekend, and you have work on Tuesday unless you took the longer version.

## Which break to choose

If you have the leave, the ten-day version after Easter (26 March to 4 April, four leave days) is usually the better deal than the one before it: the first days of April are a little warmer, and you travel back when the holiday crowds already have. If you only want the long weekend, pick a city by train within five hours, book now and leave on Friday morning.
